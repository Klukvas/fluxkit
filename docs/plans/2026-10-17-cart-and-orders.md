## Goal
A shopper (guest or signed-in customer) fills a cart that always shows current server prices and availability, then places an order that reserves stock, stores an immutable snapshot of what was bought and waits for payment ("awaiting payment"). Customers register, sign in and see their order history; guests reach their order with a private link. Admins list, read and cancel orders and mark them paid or fulfilled by hand. Unpaid orders expire and give their stock back. Payment, shipping and email providers can later plug in through extension points without editing core.

### Out of scope (each gets its own plan)
Payment providers and online payment flows; shipping providers, shipping methods and shipping cost; email delivery (order confirmation, email verification, password reset by email); discounts, coupons and the pricing pipeline (adjuster registry); tax calculation; refunds and returns; multi-currency selling; customer groups and B2B prices; customer address book; wishlist, reviews and other optional features; storefront and admin UI screens; order editing after placement; splitting one order line over several stock locations; OAuth, social login, two-factor authentication; a Redis-backed (multi-instance) rate limiter; GDPR export and account erasure (see Open questions).

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-16)
1. **Scope**: cart and order WITHOUT payment. Guest cart, server-side totals, order placement with status `awaiting_payment`, an immutable snapshot (title, SKU, price, image) in order items, stock reservation. Payment and shipping providers are later plans that plug in; this plan only leaves the hooks they need (status transitions, an idempotent "mark paid" entry point, events), no provider code.
2. **Who can order**: guests and customer accounts from the start (registration, login, order history). Customer auth is its own step, reusing the admin-auth ideas (hashed session token in Postgres, HttpOnly cookie, scrypt, CSRF header, lockout, rate limit) as a SEPARATE principal and cookie: no shared sessions, no way to reach `/admin` with a customer session. A guest cart is merged into the customer cart at login. Email verification and password reset by email are decided below (out, with a TODO).
3. **Stock**: adding to the cart reserves nothing. Placing the order reserves for a short TTL (default 30 minutes, configurable) through `ReservationsService`; payment commits, cancellation or expiry releases. The cart shows the current availability status, never a quantity.
4. **Prices**: the cart always shows the CURRENT catalog price, recomputed on every read, with a per-line "price changed" or "no longer available" notice. At placement the order stores an immutable snapshot (title, SKU, unit price, compare-at price, image, option values text, quantity, line total, currency) and the totals are computed by core money functions.

## Assumptions to confirm (my defaults; approving this plan approves them)

### Core or feature (AGENTS.md section 2.1)
- Cart, checkout, orders and customers are **core** (no feature flag): AGENTS.md lists them as core, and every shop needs them. Guest checkout and customer accounts are both always on; a client who wants "accounts only" or "guests only" gets a config flag later (not now: a new flag is a red zone and nobody asked).
- Payment providers, shipping providers and an email notifier are **plugins** (`packages/providers/<name>` or `packages/features/<name>`). They attach only through the extension points in "Events and extension points": domain events, two ports (`OrderPaymentsPort`, `OrderReadPort`) and the totals adjustment lines. Core never imports them; removing one leaves orders working (an order then stays `awaiting_payment` until an admin marks it paid or it expires).

### Cart
- **Cart token and cookie**: a guest cart is created lazily by the first write (a read never creates a row, so crawlers and `GET` floods create nothing). Token: 32 random bytes (base64url), only its SHA-256 is stored (`cart.token_hash`), like admin sessions. Cookie `fluxkit_cart`: `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/store`, `Secure` unless `STORE_COOKIE_SECURE=false`, `Max-Age` = `CART_TTL_DAYS`. Rationale: the cart is a bearer secret but low value; `Lax` keeps the cart when a shopper arrives from a link on another site (`Strict` would show an empty cart on the first navigation); writes are protected by the CSRF header anyway.
- **Signed-in customers** have one cart bound to their account (`cart.customer_id`, unique); the cart cookie is ignored while a customer session is present and is cleared at login after the merge.
- **TTL**: a cart expires `CART_TTL_DAYS` (default 30) after its last write; customer carts too (same rule, one job). Expired carts are deleted by the scheduler (no stock is held, so nothing to release).
- **Limits** (core constants, not contract): at most 100 lines per cart, quantity 1 to 999 per line (far below `MAX_QUANTITY`; protects against DoS and overflow: 999 x `MAX_AMOUNT` is refused as `line_total_out_of_range`, see Core). One line per variant: adding a variant already in the cart adds to its quantity; going above 999 is refused (`quantity_limit`), never silently capped. Adding a variant that is not sellable is refused (`variant_not_sellable`).
- **Price notices**: each line stores the unit price the shopper last saw (`seen_unit_amount`, `seen_currency`), set when the line is added or the quantity changes and by `POST /store/cart/acknowledge`. A read compares it with the current price and returns `price_changed {previousAmount}`; a variant that is no longer sellable returns `unavailable` (line kept, excluded from the subtotal); `out_of_stock` comes from the availability status. The notice stays until acknowledged or the line is changed.
- **Merge at login or registration**: guest lines are added to the customer's cart in one transaction (both carts locked in id order); quantities add up and are capped at 999 with a `quantity_capped` notice in the response (here capping is right: the shopper did not type the sum); lines beyond 100 are dropped with `lines_dropped`; the guest cart is deleted. `seen_unit_amount` takes the guest value when the customer cart had no such line, otherwise the older one (so a change shows).

### Checkout and orders
- **Required fields**: `email` (normalized like admin emails; never verified), `phone` (required: Ukrainian carriers need it; loose generic validation: optional leading `+`, 7 to 15 digits after removing spaces, dashes, dots and parentheses; stored as `+digits` or `digits`, no country inference), `firstName`, `lastName` (1 to 100 characters each), `shippingAddress` required when any line's product type has `requires_shipping = true`; for an order of digital goods only it is optional (accepted and stored when sent). `billingAddress` optional. `locale` optional (default `DEFAULT_LOCALE`), stored for later notifications.
- **Address shape** (generic, no country rules in core): `{countryCode (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, upper case, pattern only), region?, city, postalCode?, line1, line2?, recipientFirstName?, recipientLastName?, recipientPhone?}`, text lengths 1 to 200 (postal code 1 to 20), stored as JSONB on the order. Ukrainian carrier branches ("Nova Poshta warehouse no. 12") do not fit a street address: the shipping plan adds a provider-specific `shippingDetails` object; `line1` carries free text until then.
- **Order number**: 8 characters of Crockford base32 from 40 random bits, shown as `XXXX-XXXX` (for example `7K3M-9QX2`). Human-friendly (no `I`, `L`, `O`, `U`; input is normalized: lower case, `O`->`0`, `I`/`L`->`1`, dash optional), not sequential (does not reveal sales volume), and race-free (unique constraint; on a unique violation the placement transaction retries with a new number, at most 5 times, then fails loudly). The number is NOT a secret: reading an order always needs a session or an access token. Generation is pure in core (random bytes passed in); the API supplies `crypto.randomBytes`. A shop prefix is not added (it would be client branding).
- **Statuses** (`awaiting_payment`, `paid`, `fulfilled`, `cancelled`, `expired`) and transitions, enforced only through the core state machine and recorded in an append-only status journal:

| From | To | Who | Stock effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| (new) | `awaiting_payment` | checkout | reservations created (all or nothing) |
| `awaiting_payment` | `paid` | `markPaid` (admin by hand now; a payment plugin later) | commit every reservation (see open question 3 for backorder) |
| `awaiting_payment` | `cancelled` | the customer who owns it, the guest with the access token, an admin (`orders.write`) | release every reservation |
| `awaiting_payment` | `expired` | the scheduler, when `expires_at <= now` | release every reservation |
| `paid` | `fulfilled` | admin (`orders.write`) by hand now; a shipping plugin later | commit any reservation still active (backorder) or refuse `awaiting_stock` |
| `paid` | `cancelled` | admin only, with a reason (see open question 4) | none automatic (sold units stay sold; restock is an inventory `return` adjustment); refund outside the system |

  `fulfilled`, `cancelled` and `expired` are terminal. Every other pair is refused (`invalid_transition`). ADR 0004 decision 4 says the order status is derived from separate payment and fulfillment entities; v1 keeps one explicit status because neither entity exists yet. The payment plan may split it (ADR 0017 records this; see open question 9).
- **Who can cancel**: the owner of the order (customer session, or guest with the access token) only while `awaiting_payment`; admins in the table above.
- **Totals**: `subtotal` = sum of line totals (core `multiplyByQuantity` and `addMoney`, exact, no rounding). Shipping, discount and tax are **adjustment lines** (`order_adjustment`: `kind`, `code`, `label`, signed `amount`, `includedInPrice`) that v1 never creates; `total = subtotal + sum(adjustments not included in price)`. The order also stores the cached sums `shipping_total`, `discount_total`, `tax_total` (0 in v1) and `total`. Later plans add adjustment lines without a migration. With `PRICES_INCLUDE_TAX=false` no tax is added (open question 11).
- **Price drift between cart and checkout**: the checkout request carries `expectedTotal {amount, currency}` (the total the shopper saw). The server recomputes everything and never uses it as a price; if it differs, or any line has `unavailable`, checkout answers `409 cart_changed` with the fresh cart, and nothing is reserved.
- **Idempotency**: `POST /store/checkout` requires an `Idempotency-Key` header (a UUID the client generates per checkout attempt). The key is stored on the order (unique) with a SHA-256 fingerprint of the normalized request plus the cart id. Same key and same fingerprint -> the same order is returned (`200` instead of `201`); same key and a different fingerprint -> `409 idempotency_key_reused`. A concurrent duplicate waits on the unique index, then replays. The cart row is locked `FOR UPDATE` first, so two different keys for one cart serialize and the second sees an empty cart (`409 cart_empty`). Keys are kept as long as the order (no separate table, no expiry).
- **Guest access**: placement returns an `accessToken` (32 random bytes, base64url, only its SHA-256 is stored on the order; valid `ORDER_GUEST_ACCESS_DAYS`, default 90) once. The storefront puts it in the confirmation link in the URL **fragment** (`/orders/7K3M-9QX2#t=...`, never sent to servers or in `Referer`) and calls `POST /store/orders/lookup {number, accessToken}` (a POST, so the token is not in URLs or access logs). An idempotent replay rotates the token (the old one stops working) because the raw token is never stored; the replaying client is the one that lost the first response. A customer's own orders need no token (session). Guest orders are never attached to an account by email (the email is unverified).
- **Partial reservation failure**: all or nothing in ONE transaction. Lines are reserved in a fixed order (sorted by variant id); the first refusal rolls everything back and the response lists every refused line (`409 checkout_refused`, `errors[]` with `path: lines.N` and `code`: `insufficient_stock`, `variant_not_sellable`, `reserved_out_of_range`, `line_total_out_of_range`). To report every line, lines are evaluated against the locked levels first, then reserved only when all pass. Untracked variants (`not_tracked`) get no reservation and always pass.
- **Re-validation before payment**: placement locks the product rows `FOR SHARE` (sorted by id), then the variants `FOR SHARE`, then the levels `FOR UPDATE`, so an unpublish, a price write or a variant switch-off either finishes before the checkout reads (and is seen) or waits until the order is committed. Once placed, the snapshot is the contract: `markPaid` does not re-check sellability or price. This closes the race left open in `docs/api/inventory.md` ("checkout validates again before payment").
- **Stock location**: v1 reserves at the default location only (one reservation per order line). The store's availability sums all locations; while only `main` exists (locations are read-only in the API) they agree. Multi-location allocation is a later plan; the link table `order_item_reservation` already allows several reservations per line, so it needs no migration.
- **Reservation TTL and order expiry**: an order's `expires_at` = placement + `ORDER_PAYMENT_TTL_MINUTES` (default 30, 5 to 1440). Its reservations get `expires_at` = order expiry + a fixed 10-minute grace, so the order job always expires the order (and releases its reservations) before the generic reservation sweep could touch them; the sweep only cleans orphans. `markPaid` and the expiry job both lock the order row first, so exactly one of them wins.
- **Late payment**: `markPaid` on an `expired` or `cancelled` order is refused (`order_not_payable`) and changes nothing; the caller (admin, later a payment plugin) handles the refund. See open question 5.

### Scheduler (new; needed by `expireDue`, order expiry, cart cleanup, events)
- **Default: an in-process interval in the API** (`SCHEDULER_ENABLED`, default `true`; `SCHEDULER_INTERVAL_SECONDS`, default 60). Each tick takes a Postgres advisory lock (`pg_try_advisory_lock`) so only one API instance runs the jobs; jobs: expire due orders, `ReservationsService.expireDue` looped on `nextCursor` (alerting as an error log on `failures`), delete expired carts in batches, delete expired customer sessions, dispatch pending domain events. Each job is also a CLI (`pnpm jobs:run-once`) for tests and for a system cron.
- Rationale: no worker image or deployment exists yet (AGENTS.md "API/worker images are not built yet"), `REDIS_URL` is optional today, and Postgres is the only service every installation has. BullMQ is approved in the stack and installed, so moving the same job functions into a BullMQ repeatable job in a worker later needs **no new dependency**; the job functions take `now` and are transport-agnostic. **No new dependency is requested by this plan.** See open question 2.

### Customers
- **Separate principal**: tables `customer` and `customer_session` (no link to `admin_user`), cookie `fluxkit_customer_session` with `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/store`, `Secure` unless `STORE_COOKIE_SECURE=false`. The admin guard reads only `fluxkit_admin_session` (Path `/admin`), the customer guard only the customer cookie; a test proves a customer cookie on any `/admin` route is `401` and an admin cookie on customer routes is `401`. The same email may exist as an admin and as a customer; they are unrelated accounts.
- **Passwords and sessions**: the same scrypt hasher, password policy (12 to 128 characters, not equal to the email) and session expiry rules as admins, with customer limits: idle `CUSTOMER_SESSION_IDLE_MINUTES` (default 43 200 = 30 days), absolute `CUSTOMER_SESSION_MAX_HOURS` (default 2 160 = 90 days). A new token at every login and registration (no session fixation); password change revokes the other sessions; deactivation by an admin revokes all.
- **Lockout and limits**: 5 failed logins lock the account for 15 minutes (as ADR 0010); identical `401` for unknown email, wrong password, locked or inactive account. Per-IP limits use the existing in-memory window class (generalized, single-process limitation documented): login 20 per 10 minutes, registration 10 per hour, checkout 10 per 10 minutes, order lookup and guest cancel 20 per 10 minutes, cart writes 120 per minute. Numbers are internal, not contract. `429 too_many_requests` with `Retry-After`.
- **Email verification and password reset: OUT**, marked `TODO(fluxkit): needs the email provider plan`. The schema already has `customer.email_verified_at` (always null in v1) so the later plan needs no migration for it. No "mail port" is built now (an unused port is speculative); the email plan defines it. A customer who forgets the password can still check out as a guest; an admin cannot set a customer's password (only deactivate or unlock). See open questions 7 and 8.
- **Registration and enumeration**: without email delivery, registration with a taken email must say so (`409 conflict` on `email`), which reveals that the email has an account. Accepted trade-off for v1, mitigated by the registration rate limit; the email plan switches to "check your inbox" answers. Login stays non-enumerating.
- **Profile**: `firstName`, `lastName`, `phone` (optional). No address book in v1 (the order stores its addresses); ADR 0004's `address` entity comes with the storefront plan if wanted.

### Privacy, retention, other
- **PII minimisation**: orders store email, phone, names and addresses (needed to deliver and for accounting); customers store email, names, optional phone. No IP address, user agent or device data is stored anywhere. Logs never contain emails, phones, addresses, tokens, idempotency keys or order access links (the M3 lesson: query errors are logged by class and code only). Domain event payloads carry ids and the order number only, never PII.
- **Retention**: no automatic deletion of orders or customers in v1 (accounting needs orders; the period is a legal decision). Deactivation only; deleting a customer sets `sales_order.customer_id` to null (the snapshot keeps the contact data). See open question 10.
- **Currency**: the shop currency only. A cart line priced in another currency (after a `SHOP_CURRENCY` change) shows `price_changed`.
- **Customer groups**: out (ADR 0004 keeps them for a B2B plan).
- **Abandoned carts**: deleted after `CART_TTL_DAYS`; no reminder emails (email plan).
- **Store CORS**: the API keeps sending no CORS headers. The storefront calls the API same-site (Next.js server-side calls or a reverse proxy path); see open question 1.

## Design

### Core (pure TypeScript, TDD, `packages/core/src/cart`, `packages/core/src/orders`, reuse of `auth` and `pricing`)
- **Cart rules** (`cart/`): `addLine(lines, variantId, quantity)`, `setLineQuantity`, `removeLine`, `mergeCarts(customerLines, guestLines)` with the limits above and typed results (`invalid_quantity`, `quantity_limit`, `too_many_lines`); `mergeCarts` returns the merged lines plus notices (`quantity_capped`, `lines_dropped`).
- **Cart pricing** (`cart/`): `priceCart(lines with facts {sellable, currentPrice, availability, seen})` -> per-line `unitPrice`, `lineTotal` (`multiplyByQuantity`), notices, `subtotal` (`addMoney` over sellable lines), `purchasable` (no `unavailable` line, at least one line). Overflow is a typed refusal (`line_total_out_of_range`, `subtotal_out_of_range`), never a clamp. Tests first, including 999 x `MAX_AMOUNT`, currency mismatch, zero lines, a line in another currency.
- **Order totals** (`orders/totals.ts`): `computeOrderTotals(lines, adjustments)` -> `{subtotal, shippingTotal, discountTotal, taxTotal, total}`; adjustments with `includedInPrice` are reported but not added; a negative total is refused (`negative_total`). Tests against an independent BigInt reference, as the money module.
- **Snapshot** (`orders/snapshot.ts`): `buildOrderLine(facts)` validates and freezes `{productId, variantId, sku, productTitle (i18n map), variantTitle (i18n map or null), optionValues [{option (i18n), value (i18n)}], imageUrl, unitPrice, compareAtAmount, quantity, lineTotal, requiresShipping}`; texts are stored as full locale maps so history renders in any locale.
- **Status machine** (`orders/status.ts`): the transition table above as data; `decideTransition(from, to, actor)` with actor `customer`, `guest`, `admin`, `system`; typed refusals `invalid_transition`, `not_allowed_for_actor`; `stockEffectOf(from, to)` (`reserve`, `commit`, `release`, `none`).
- **Order number** (`orders/number.ts`): `formatOrderNumber(bytes: Uint8Array)` (exactly 5 bytes), `parseOrderNumber(text)` (normalization, typed `invalid_order_number`).
- **Checkout input** (`orders/contact.ts`): address validation, phone normalization, required-shipping rule `shippingAddressRequired(lines)`.
- **Expiry and access**: `orderExpiresAt(placedAt, ttlMinutes)`, `reservationExpiresAt(orderExpiresAt)` (+ grace), `isGuestAccessValid(createdAt, now, days)`.
- **Customer auth**: reuse `packages/core/src/auth` (`normalizeEmail`, password policy, lockout, `evaluateSession`) unchanged; only add customer session limits defaults. No admin role or permission applies to customers.
- **Admin permissions**: `PERMISSIONS` gains `orders.read`, `orders.write`, `customers.read`, `customers.write`; `ROLE_PERMISSIONS`: `owner` all, `manager` all four new ones, `viewer` none of them (PII; open question 6). No migration (roles and permissions live in code; the `admin_user.role` CHECK is unchanged). Tests in `roles.spec.ts`.

### Data (migration `0004_cart_customers_orders`, up and down)
Naming follows `catalog.ts` and `pricing-inventory.ts` (explicit `_key`, `_idx`, `_check`, `_fkey` names). `order` is an SQL keyword, so the table is `sales_order`; its children keep the `order_` prefix.
- `customer`: `id`, `email` text unique (CHECK `email = lower(btrim(email))`, closing the admin follow-up for this table), `password_hash`, `first_name`, `last_name` (1 to 100), `phone` null, `is_active` (default true), `email_verified_at` null, `failed_login_count` int default 0, `locked_until` null, `last_login_at` null, `password_changed_at`, timestamps.
- `customer_session`: `id`, `customer_id` FK `ON DELETE CASCADE`, `token_hash` unique, `created_at`, `last_seen_at`, `expires_at`; index (`customer_id`), index (`expires_at`).
- `cart`: `id`, `token_hash` text null unique, `customer_id` FK null unique `ON DELETE CASCADE`, CHECK exactly one of the two is set, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `expires_at`; index (`expires_at`).
- `cart_item`: `id`, `cart_id` FK `ON DELETE CASCADE`, `variant_id` FK `ON DELETE CASCADE` (a deleted variant leaves the cart; only never-sold variants can be deleted, ADR 0013), `quantity` 1 to 999, `seen_unit_amount` bigint null (0 to `MAX_AMOUNT`), `seen_currency` null (CHECK both or neither), timestamps; unique (`cart_id`, `variant_id`); index (`variant_id`).
- `sales_order`: `id`, `number` unique (CHECK Crockford pattern), `status` CHECK in the five statuses, `customer_id` FK null `ON DELETE SET NULL`, `email`, `phone`, `first_name`, `last_name`, `shipping_address` jsonb null, `billing_address` jsonb null, `locale`, `currency` (pattern), `subtotal_amount`, `shipping_total`, `discount_total`, `tax_total`, `total_amount` (bigint, CHECKs: 0 to `MAX_AMOUNT`, discount 0 or negative), `access_token_hash` null unique and `access_expires_at` null (set for guest orders only; a customer order has none, and keeps none when its customer is deleted), `idempotency_key` uuid unique, `request_fingerprint` text, `placed_at`, `expires_at`, `paid_at`, `fulfilled_at`, `cancelled_at`, `expired_at` (null), `cancellation_reason` null (max 500), `payment_reference` null (max 255, set by `markPaid`), timestamps. Indexes: (`customer_id`, `placed_at` desc), (`status`, `expires_at`) for the expiry job, (`placed_at` desc) for the admin list, (`email`) for admin search.
- `order_item`: `id`, `order_id` FK `ON DELETE CASCADE`, `position`, `product_id` FK null `ON DELETE SET NULL`, `variant_id` FK null `ON DELETE SET NULL`, `sku`, `product_title` jsonb, `variant_title` jsonb null, `option_values` jsonb, `image_url` null, `unit_amount`, `compare_at_amount` null, `quantity` (1 to 999), `line_total`, `currency`, `requires_shipping`, `created_at`. CHECKs mirror core limits; CHECK `line_total = unit_amount * quantity`.
  **Immutable**: a `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger in the ADR 0012 style rejects everything except, at trigger depth above 1, the `SET NULL` of `product_id` or `variant_id` alone and the cascade delete with its order (orders are never deleted by the application). The snapshot therefore survives catalog changes and deletions.
- `order_adjustment`: `id`, `order_id` FK cascade, `kind` (CHECK pattern `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,63}$`; core knows `shipping`, `discount`, `tax`; plugins may add kinds without a migration), `code`, `label` jsonb, `amount` bigint (signed, within `MAX_AMOUNT`), `included_in_price` bool, `source` text (plugin name), `created_at`. Append-only trigger. Empty in v1.
- `order_status_change`: append-only journal: `id`, `order_id` FK cascade, `from_status` null, `to_status`, `actor_type` (`customer`, `guest`, `admin`, `system`), `actor_admin_id` FK null `ON DELETE SET NULL` (same depth-guarded exception as `stock_movement.actor_id`), `reason` null, `created_at` (`clock_timestamp()`). Append-only trigger.
- `order_item_reservation`: (`order_item_id` FK cascade, `reservation_id` FK `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, unique) primary key on both. A reservation is only ever referenced by one order line; `reservation.reference` also carries the order number for the journal.
- `domain_event` (outbox): `id`, `type` (pattern), `aggregate_id` uuid, `payload` jsonb (ids only), `created_at`, `dispatched_at` null, `attempts` int, `last_error_code` null; index (`dispatched_at`, `created_at`) where not dispatched. Written in the same transaction as the change it reports. Dispatched rows are deleted after 7 days by the scheduler.
- Down migration drops the tables, the trigger functions and its row in `drizzle.__drizzle_migrations`; tested up, down, up, down with data in the tables.
- Lock order (documented in ADR 0015 and in code comments): checkout `cart` -> `product` rows (`FOR SHARE`, by id) -> `variant` rows (`FOR SHARE`, by id) -> `inventory_level` (`FOR UPDATE`, by variant id) -> inserts. Order transitions: `sales_order` (`FOR UPDATE`) -> levels -> reservations. Admin writes keep product -> level and product -> variant; reservations keep variant -> level. No cycle.

### Events and extension points (new contract `docs/contracts/events.md`)
- **Domain events** (outbox `domain_event`, written in the same transaction, dispatched at least once by the scheduler; handlers must be idempotent by event id): `order.placed`, `order.paid`, `order.cancelled`, `order.expired`, `order.fulfilled`, `customer.registered`. Payload `{eventId, type, occurredAt, orderId | customerId, orderNumber?, status?}`; no PII (a handler reads what it needs through `OrderReadPort`).
- **Handler registration without imports**: the event names, payload types, port interfaces and the metadata key `fluxkit:domain-event-handler` are exported by `@fluxkit/core` (types and constants only, still pure). A plugin's Nest provider marks itself with that metadata (`SetMetadata` from `@nestjs/common`, which plugins may use); the API discovers marked providers with Nest `DiscoveryService` at startup. A handler that throws is retried with backoff (attempts and error code stored, no message text); after 10 attempts it is logged as an error and left for an operator.
- **Ports** (interfaces in core, implementations in `apps/api`, injected by string tokens exported from core): `OrderPaymentsPort.markPaid(orderId, {amount, providerReference, source})` (idempotent: the same reference on a paid order answers ok; a different reference answers `payment_reference_conflict`; amount must equal the total) and `OrderReadPort.getOrder(orderId)` (the admin view without reservation internals).
- **Totals adjustment lines**: `order_adjustment` (kinds `shipping`, `discount`, `tax`, plugin kinds) and `computeOrderTotals`. v1 never creates one; the pricing-pipeline plan adds the adjuster registry that fills them at checkout.
- **How a payment plugin attaches later**: it registers its own routes (for example `/store/payments/<provider>/start` and a webhook), reads the order through `OrderReadPort`, verifies the provider signature, and calls `OrderPaymentsPort.markPaid`. **Shipping plugin**: adds a shipping adjustment at checkout (pipeline plan), reacts to `order.paid`, and moves `paid -> fulfilled` through a port added by the shipping plan. **Email notifier**: handles `order.placed`, `order.paid`, `order.cancelled`, `customer.registered`. None of them edits core.

### Security and PII
- Never leaves the store API: other customers' data, reservation ids and statuses, stock quantities, admin actors, internal ids of other rows, idempotency keys, token hashes, `request_fingerprint`, `failed_login_count`, `locked_until`, `password_hash`.
- Owner checks in SQL on every customer resource (`WHERE customer_id = $session`), 404 not 403 for others' orders; guest access only through `number + token` (constant-time comparison of hashes); the cart only through the session or cookie. Default deny: a customer route without the customer guard fails a route-walking test (like `admin-default-deny.spec.ts`), and every `/store/account/*` route is listed.
- IDOR tests: customer A reads, cancels and pages B's orders (404); a guest token of order X with number Y (404); a valid token after `ORDER_GUEST_ACCESS_DAYS` (404); cart cookie of one cart never shows another; customer cookie on `/admin` (401); admin cookie on `/store/account` (401).
- Session fixation: new token at login and registration; a pre-existing customer cookie is replaced. The cart cookie is cleared at login.
- Enumeration: login identical failures; order lookup identical 404s; order numbers 40 random bits; registration discloses taken emails (accepted, rate-limited; open question 7).
- Stock probing: repeated checkouts with growing quantities could find stock levels; rate limits and the 999 cap bound it (accepted, documented).
- Logs: no PII or tokens (test asserts a failed checkout logs no email or address).

## Risks
- **Checkout vs stock concurrency**: all-or-nothing in one transaction with a fixed lock order; tested with parallel checkouts of the last unit, crossing carts (deadlock), and checkout vs admin writes. A deadlock that still happens is retried once by the checkout service (Postgres `40P01`), then answers 503 with a log.
- **Double submit**: idempotency key unique plus the cart row lock; tested with parallel identical and different keys.
- **Price drift**: `expectedTotal` consent check plus the snapshot; the cart never charges a stale price, the order never changes after placement.
- **Abandoned reservations**: every reservation belongs to an order with an expiry; the order job releases them; the generic sweep catches orphans 10 minutes later; `failures` are logged as errors. If the scheduler is disabled on every instance, stock stays held: `GET /health` does not show this yet (follow-up: a scheduler heartbeat in health).
- **Scheduler on several instances**: advisory lock; a crashed holder releases it with its connection.
- **PII leakage**: leak tests on every store response, log assertions, event payloads without PII, viewer role without order access.
- **Session fixation and cookie confusion**: separate names, paths and tables; new token at login; tests.
- **Guest token theft**: the token is a bearer secret in the confirmation link fragment; it expires; a replay rotates it; it only allows reading and cancelling an unpaid order (not changing contact data).
- **Enumeration** of order numbers (random, lookup needs the token) and emails (registration only, rate-limited, accepted for v1).
- **Cart merge races**: both carts locked in id order in the login transaction; a parallel cart write waits.
- **DoS on cart size**: 100 lines, 999 per line, lazy cart creation, cart write rate limit, 100 KB body limit (existing).
- **Single-process rate limits**: as for admin login; a Redis-backed limiter is a separate plan (launch blocker already listed in HANDOFF).
- **Backorder after payment** depends on `extend` and on fulfilment committing later (open question 3).
- **Multi-location stock**: v1 reserves at the default location only; a shop that adds locations by SQL sees `in_stock` but gets `insufficient_stock` at checkout. Documented; locations are read-only in the API.
- **Scope size**: 11 PRs. The split keeps each PR reviewable; customer auth and checkout are the critical ones.

## Open questions (for the owner's quiz; recommended answer first)
1. **How does the storefront reach the API?** Recommended: same-site (Next.js server-side calls or a reverse-proxy path), no CORS in v1. Alternatives: CORS with an allow-list of storefront origins (`STORE_ALLOWED_ORIGINS`, credentials), or a separate API host with tokens instead of cookies.
2. **Where does the scheduler run?** Recommended: in-process interval in the API with a Postgres advisory lock, BullMQ worker later. Alternatives: a BullMQ repeatable job and a separate worker process now (makes `REDIS_URL` required and needs a worker entry point), or only a CLI for an external cron.
3. **Backordered lines in a paid order** (commit is refused until stock arrives). Recommended: on `markPaid` commit what is on hand, `extend` the remaining reservations by a long hold (365 days), commit them at `mark-fulfilled` (refused `awaiting_stock` until stock arrives). Alternatives: refuse backorder variants at checkout in v1, or commit at fulfilment for every line.
4. **Cancelling a paid order**. Recommended: admin only, with a reason; stock is not returned automatically (admin records a `return` adjustment); refund outside the system until the refunds plan. Alternatives: automatic restock on cancel, or no cancel after payment until refunds exist.
5. **Payment arrives for an expired or cancelled order**. Recommended: refuse (`order_not_payable`), nothing changes, the payment plugin or admin refunds. Alternatives: accept and try to re-reserve (paid without stock if that fails), or keep the order open longer than the payment provider's session (the payment plan must guarantee its session TTL is shorter than `ORDER_PAYMENT_TTL_MINUTES` anyway).
6. **Viewer role and customer data**. Recommended: `viewer` gets neither orders nor customers (PII); `manager` gets all four new permissions. Alternatives: `viewer` gets `orders.read`, or a new fixed role `support` (`orders.*`, `customers.read`).
7. **Registration discloses that an email is taken** (no email delivery). Recommended: accept for v1 with a rate limit; switch to "check your inbox" with the email plan. Alternative: registration disabled until email delivery exists (guest checkout only).
8. **Forgotten customer passwords before email delivery**. Recommended: no recovery in v1 (guest checkout still works; admin can only deactivate or unlock). Alternative: an admin-issued one-time reset link that the shop sends by hand.
9. **Single order status vs derived payment and fulfilment statuses (ADR 0004, cash on delivery)**. Recommended: one status machine now; the payment plan decides the split (cash on delivery needs `fulfilled` before `paid`) in its own ADR. Alternative: add `payment_status` and `fulfillment_status` columns now.
10. **Retention and erasure of personal data**. Recommended: no automatic deletion in v1, deactivation only; a privacy plan (anonymization after the legal period, account erasure, export) before the EU launch. Alternative: anonymize guest order contact data after N years now (needs the legal period).
11. **Tax with `PRICES_INCLUDE_TAX=false`** (no tax calculation exists). Recommended: allowed; `taxTotal` is 0 and the docs warn that tax is not charged until the tax plan. Alternative: refuse to start checkout when the flag is `false`.
12. **Guest orders and later accounts**. Recommended: never attach guest orders to an account by email in v1 (unverified email). Alternative: attach after email verification exists (email plan).
