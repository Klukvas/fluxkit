## Findings

### F1 (Blocker): an unverified account claims a guest buyer's orders for export and erasure

- Where: `apps/api/src/privacy/export/personal-data.reader.ts:121-135` (`ordersCondition` and `mailCondition`: `customer_id = account OR email = account email`), used by `apps/api/src/privacy/export/personal-data-export.service.ts:100-126` (`selfService`: the subject is `byCustomer`, nothing checks `emailVerifiedAt`); the same conditions in `apps/api/src/privacy/erasure/privacy-erasure.service.ts:90-102`, used by `apps/api/src/customers/account/account-deletion.service.ts:82-86`. Registration takes any address without verification (`apps/api/src/customers/account/account.service.ts:102-150`, "verification is optional", ADR 0031 owner answer 2) and refuses an email only when another ACCOUNT has it. ADR 0031 line 139: "Verification unlocks nothing in v1; it is the prerequisite for attaching guest orders by email later." The privacy step attached them without it.
- Scenario: a shop has guest buyers (the common case before registration). An attacker who knows a guest's address (a shop employee, a leaked order confirmation, a shared household) registers an account with it, is never asked to confirm it, and calls `GET /store/account/data-export`: the document contains every order with that email: the guest's names, phone, delivery address, lines, payments (card system and last 4), status history. Then `POST /store/account/delete` with the attacker's own password anonymises the guest's orders (their guest link stops working, their pending order mails are deleted) and records a `privacy_request` as if the subject had asked. The right of access and the right to erasure of one person are exercised by another on the strength of an unverified string.
- Proof (scratch spec R1, real routes, real Postgres; passes on the branch):
  ```
  victim guest order (cancelled) with phone +380671112233, names Victimfirst Victimlast, line1 "Victim street 7"; a pending mail to the victim
  attacker: POST /store/account/register {email: <victim email>} -> 201; GET /store/account/me -> customer.emailVerified === false
  attacker: GET /store/account/data-export -> 200; orders[] contains the victim's number with
     contact {phone: "+380671112233", firstName: "Victimfirst", lastName: "Victimlast"}, shippingAddress.line1 "Victim street 7"
  attacker: POST /store/account/delete {password, confirm: true} -> 204
  sales_order of the victim: email null, phone null, access_token_hash null; POST /store/orders/lookup {number, accessToken} -> 404
  email_message of the victim: 0 rows
  ```
- Suggested fix (rules first, core and ADR): the self-service subject is the ACCOUNT only (`customer_id = me`) unless the address is verified (`email_verified_at IS NOT NULL`), and even then only guest orders (`customer_id IS NULL`, see F2) and guest mail rows (`customer_id IS NULL`). Decide in a pure core rule (`subjectScope(account)`), test it with a failing test first, and have the API spec register an unverified account over an existing guest order and assert that the export has 0 guest orders and the deletion changes none. Alternatively require verification for `data-export` and `delete` (`409 rule_violation email_not_verified`). Record the decision in ADR 0033 (it supersedes the "every order with its email" sentence of decision 6) and in `docs/api/privacy.md`. Consider also refusing registration while an unverified account cannot be told from a guest (out of this plan's scope).

### F2 (Major): the by-email scope reaches orders of other live accounts; the admin lookup hides them

- Where: the same `ordersCondition` (`personal-data.reader.ts:122-127`, `privacy-erasure.service.ts:90-95`): `email = subject.email` is not restricted to `customer_id IS NULL`. The checkout accepts any `email` from a signed-in customer (`apps/api/src/orders/checkout-input.ts:61-73`; `docs/api/orders.md:31` "a signed-in customer may omit it (the account email is used). Never verified."). The lookup lists guest orders with `isNull(salesOrder.customerId)` (`apps/api/src/privacy/admin/privacy-admin.service.ts:128`), the preview and the erasure do not.
- Scenario: customer A (account `a@`) checks out typing `v@` as the contact email (a typo, a relative's address, or on purpose). The owner receives a request from `v@`, looks it up (`customer: null, guestOrders: []`: nothing to erase), records an erasure request, exports (A's order with A's names and address is in the document handed to `v@`), previews (`orders: 1`), erases: A's order loses its contact while A's account is alive and still owns the order (`customer_id` unchanged); A's own order page now shows null names. The owner was never shown which order they were about to anonymise and could not tell it was another account's.
- Proof (scratch spec R2, real routes; passes on the branch):
  ```
  A registers a@; A checks out with body.email v@ (201); sales_order.customer_id === A
  POST /admin/privacy/lookup {email: v@} -> {customer: null, guestOrders: []}
  POST /admin/privacy/requests {kind: erasure, subject: {email: v@}} -> 201
  POST .../export {email: v@} -> 200, orders[0] = A's order, contact {firstName: "Afirst", lastName: "Alast"}
  POST .../erase-preview {email: v@} -> {accountFound: false, orders: 1, ordersToAnonymise: 1}
  POST .../erase {email: v@, confirm: true} -> 200
  sales_order of A: customer_id = A, email null, first_name null, last_name null
  A: GET /store/account/orders/<number> -> 200 with "firstName":null
  ```
- Suggested fix: restrict the by-email branch to guest orders (`AND customer_id IS NULL`) and guest mail rows (`AND customer_id IS NULL`) in both services (one shared condition, used by lookup, export, preview and erasure, so they can never disagree). An order of another account that carries the subject's email is that other account's record: it is reached through that account's own export and deletion, not through the address. Decide the rule in core (a pure `subjectOrdersFilter`) with a failing test first; add API specs where another account's order carries the subject's email and assert the lookup, the preview, the export and the erasure all exclude it. Update `docs/api/privacy.md:9` and `:35` and ADR 0033 decision 6.

### F3 (Minor): a stranger's open guest order with my email blocks my deletion

- Where: `privacy-erasure.service.ts:234-244` (`blockersOf` over every order of the subject, guest orders with the address included); the blocker list itself is correct for the subject's own orders.
- Scenario: anyone can place a guest order with my address and leave it unpaid (`awaiting_payment`, `ORDER_PAYMENT_TTL_MINUTES`, default 30, up to `MAX_OPEN_ORDERS` at once, repeatable without limit over time). While one exists my `POST /store/account/delete` answers `409 erasure_blocked open_order` naming an order number that is not mine. The hold is temporary (the order expires) and the shop owner can still erase me by hand, so Minor; it also leaks the fact that an order with my address exists (its number), which F1 and F2 make the smaller problem.
- Proof (scratch spec R6): `register(email)`; a guest places a pickup order with the same email; `POST /store/account/delete` -> `409 {rule: "erasure_blocked", blockers: [{orderNumber: <the stranger's>, reason: "open_order"}]}`.
- Suggested fix: follows from the F1 scope rule (an unverified account has no by-email orders). For a verified account, consider leaving `awaiting_payment` guest orders out of the self-service erasure scope (they expire on their own, the mail handler then finds no recipient only if they were anonymised, so the blocker is the right answer for the owner's manual erasure but not for the stranger's unpaid cart).

### F4 (Minor): one failing order aborts the whole job 12 tick

- Where: `apps/api/src/privacy/retention/privacy-retention.service.ts:50` (comment: "a row held by a stuck writer fails this order, not the tick"); `apps/api/src/jobs/anonymise-orders.job.ts:65-76` has no try/catch around `anonymiseIfDue`, so the exception leaves `anonymiseOrders`, the runner logs the job as failed, and the purges of that tick never run. The next tick restarts from the first candidate (the ones already anonymised are no longer candidates), reaches the same row and fails again: every order after it in id order waits until that row's lock or data problem is fixed.
- Proof (scratch spec R5, pure unit test): `candidates` -> `["a","b","c"]`, `anonymiseIfDue("b")` throws `lock timeout`: `anonymiseOrders` rejects, calls were `["a","b"]`, `c` never reached.
- Suggested fix: catch per order, count it (`failed`), log the id and code, continue; keep the throw for a failure of `candidates` itself. Or make the comment true the other way round and document the behaviour in `docs/api/jobs.md`. A spec with a throwing `anonymiseIfDue` either way.

### F5 (Minor): database-level limits of the append-only and erased-contact rules

- Where: `apps/api/drizzle/0009_privacy.sql:64-77` (row-level `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger; no `TRUNCATE` trigger; the ADR 0019 journals have none either); `apps/api/src/database/schema/orders.ts:131-134` (the contact CHECK is symmetric: a row may move from erased back to live).
- Proof (scratch spec R3 and R4, SQL on the real schema): `DELETE FROM admin_audit_event` -> `admin_audit_event is append-only`; `TRUNCATE admin_audit_event` -> 0 rows (rolled back in the test). After a real erasure, `UPDATE sales_order SET contact_erased_at = NULL, email = ..., phone = ..., first_name = ..., last_name = ...` succeeds.
- Suggested fix: optional. A statement-level `BEFORE TRUNCATE` trigger that raises (one line, same technique) would make the audit table's guarantee complete against an application-role connection; a trigger refusing `contact_erased_at` NOT NULL -> NULL would make "irreversible" true at the database too. Both are schema changes (red zone); at least record the limits in ADR 0033 and the runbook (step 11).

## Verified OK (with the method)

- Permissions: every `/admin/privacy/*` route needs `privacy.manage` (owner only; manager 403 in the erasure spec), `GET /admin/audit-events` needs `users.manage`; the route walk `admin-permissions.spec.ts:59-70, 141, 421-429` lists them; `privacy.manage` is in no other role (core `roles.spec.ts`). Store routes: `data-export` and `delete` are in the customer-route walk (`customer-routes.spec.ts:65`), anonymous 401. No IDOR surface: request ids are uuids and every request route is owner-only; a completed erasure's stored result is answered only to the owner.
- Re-authentication of the deletion (scratch spec R11): without `X-Fluxkit-CSRF` the route answers 403 (global `StoreCsrfGuard`); 10 wrong passwords lock the account (`locked_until` set, the failed-sign-in counter is the login's); the RIGHT password is then refused (400, dummy scrypt) and the account still exists; the session cookie is cleared on success; the second of two parallel deletions is 401 (author's spec).
- Erasure integrity (author's specs plus the review's reading): the anonymisation writes NULL to the four contact columns and `cancellation_reason`, `{countryCode}` to both addresses, NULL to the guest access pair, a fresh random fingerprint, `contact_erased_at`; lines, adjustments, journal, shipping snapshot, shipments and payments compare equal before and after; the stranger's order is untouched; the guest link answers 404; `email_message` rows by customer and by address are deleted; a `sending` row under a live lease blocks; a checkout replay with the key of an erased guest order answers `409 idempotency_key_reused` (fingerprint replaced), never a 500 on the erased-access CHECK (scratch spec R10). Journals that keep admin free text (`order_status_change.reason`, `payment_refund.note`, `stock_movement.reason`) are kept by design (plan D12, D15, D21); `order_shipping.destination` holds pickup-point data only (the address method stores none); `payment`, `payment_event`, `shipment` hold no name, phone or address (schema comments and the inventory); `domain_event` payloads are ids (sentinel spec).
- Blockers that are tested through the API: `open_order` (self-service and admin), `active_shipment` (created shipment), `mail_sending`; core `decideErasure` walks every status of the four machines (`packages/core/src/privacy/erasure.spec.ts`).
